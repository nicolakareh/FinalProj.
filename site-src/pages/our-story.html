Our story

Four generations of produce people.

The Fruit Center has been a family business for more than fifty years. The family has been in produce for well over a hundred.

Est.1973

The rule we run on

People first, product second. But of course, it's a very close second.

The Mignosa family's standing rule

Don Mignosa opened the first Fruit Center in Weymouth in 1973, then Hingham in 1976 and Milton in 1979. His sons Mark and Michael grew up in the stores and run them today, Mark in Hingham and Michael in Milton, with a plaza of four shops that has become the heartbeat of East Milton Square.

The stores have grown into full markets, with a butcher, a fish counter, a deli, a kitchen, a sushi chef, a wine department, and a florist. The produce is still the reason people drive past three other grocery stores to get here. And the rule hasn't changed: take care of people, and take care of the product, in that order and by a hair.

As Mark puts it, "their parents shopped here, and then their kids."

Timeline

From a fruit stand to two stores

  1. 1889

    A fruit stand on West Broadway

    Carmelo "Charles" Mignosa, who came from Sicily as a teenager, opens Mignosa's Fruit Stand in South Boston. He'll raise twelve children in the business.

  2. 1973

    The first Fruit Center

    Don Mignosa, one of Carmelo's twelve, opens a store called the Fruit Center in Weymouth. The name says exactly what it sells.

  3. 1976

    Hingham, across from the harbor

    The Hingham store opens on Water Street at the corner of Route 3A. Today it's managed by Don's son Mark.

  4. 1979

    Milton joins

    The Milton store opens in East Milton Square, first across the street from where it stands now, and later grows into today's market and plaza under Don's son Michael.

  5. 2004

    Rocky Neck takes the fish counter

    Rocky Neck Fish begins owning and managing the fresh fish departments, buying directly from fishermen as both a wholesaler and a retailer.

  6. 2008

    Mike's Fresh Sushi

    Mike's joins the Fruit Center and starts rolling every morning. Sushi becomes a South Shore grocery staple.

  7. 2013

    Forty years

    Hingham and Milton celebrate four decades of the Fruit Center, and the stores have grown into full markets with a butcher, a kitchen, wine, and flowers.

  8. 2023

    Fifty years

    The Fruit Center marks a half century of serving the South Shore with celebrations in both stores, and thirteen straight years on The Boston Globe's Top Places to Work list.

  9. Today

    Third generation, same rule

    Mark and Michael run two roughly 12,000-square-foot stores with eleven departments each, more than a hundred local partnerships, and a produce section that still sets the standard.

Community & environment

Good neighbors, on purpose

Every year the Fruit Center contributes to more than 100 local organizations: teams, schools, churches, fundraisers, and the people who ask nicely. And because a produce market makes a lot of green waste, we've worked hard to keep it out of the landfill.

  • Solar on the roofSolar panels at the Milton store have cut its electricity spending by about 25%.
  • Everything compostedAll organic waste from every department, flowers included, is picked up by Black Earth Compost and turned into compost.
  • Recycle, reuse, donateDual-stream recycling, pallets reused with our suppliers, food donations, local purchasing, and energy-efficiency upgrades throughout the stores.
  • Bring a bagMilton and Hingham have both banned single-use plastic bags. Reusable bags welcome, always.
